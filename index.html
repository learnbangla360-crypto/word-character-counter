    Expert tips and common mistakes

    Know what counts toward the limit

    Many word limits exclude the title, references, footnotes or captions. Many include them. Read the brief, then paste only the text that counts. A few extra seconds here prevents a rejected submission.

    Different tools count differently

    A word processor, a learning platform and this page can each give slightly different totals, mostly because of dashes, slashes and symbols. If the person receiving your text will check it with their own tool, check the number there as well.

    Characters with spaces vs. without

    Social platforms and text-message limits count spaces. Some translation and academic rules don't. Use the card that matches the rule you're following.

    Meta descriptions are measured in pixels, not just characters

    Search engines cut off titles and descriptions by display width, not a fixed character count, so the 60 and 155 guidelines are approximate. Aim a little under them and put your key words early.

    Text messages can have a smaller limit than you expect

    A single standard text allows 160 characters, but messages with emoji or non-Latin letters often fall to 70 characters per segment. If your message uses them, leave extra room.

    Don't chase keyword percentages

    The repeated words list is for spotting overused terms, not for hitting a magic density. Write for the reader, and use a synonym when one word appears too often.

    Clean up duplicates before counting

    If you've pasted a list that repeats lines, the totals will be inflated. Run it through Remove Duplicate Lines first. If you want to see what changed between two drafts, the Text Diff Checker shows it line by line.

    Troubleshooting

    The count looks too high or too low

    Check whether your source includes headers, references or captions you didn't mean to paste. Also remember that rules differ between tools. This one counts space-separated pieces that contain a letter or number.

    Paste text doesn't work

    Some browsers block one-click paste. Click inside the box and press Ctrl+V (Cmd+V on a Mac) instead.

    My file won't load

    Make sure it's 2 MB or smaller and is a plain text type: .txt, .md, .csv, .json or .html. Word, PDF and image files aren't supported. For those, copy the text out and paste it into the box.

    Strange characters appear after loading a file

    The file probably isn't saved as UTF-8. Open it in a text editor, save it with UTF-8 encoding, and load it again.

    Chinese, Japanese or Thai text shows very few words

    These languages are usually written without spaces, so a run of characters counts as one word. Use the character counts instead.

    The sentence count seems off

    Abbreviations such as e.g. or Dr. can add extra sentence breaks, and text with no end punctuation counts as one sentence. Treat the sentence count as an estimate.

    The page feels slow with a very large text

    The stats refresh each time the text changes. Pause for a moment after pasting very large text, or split it into parts.

    Copy text doesn't work

    Some browsers block clipboard access in certain settings. Click inside the box, press Ctrl+A (Cmd+A on a Mac), and copy with the keyboard.

    Glossary

    Word count
    The number of words in a text. Different tools define a word slightly differently.
    Character count
    The number of characters in a text. It can include or exclude spaces.
    Whitespace
    Spaces, tabs and line breaks. They separate words and are removed in the no-spaces count.
    Code point
    A single numbered character in the Unicode standard. This tool counts characters by code points, so most emoji count as one.
    Reading time
    An estimate of how long a text takes to read silently, found by dividing word count by reading speed.
    Speaking time
    An estimate of how long a text takes to say aloud. People speak more slowly than they read.
    Keyword density
    How often a word appears compared with the total words in a text, shown as a percentage.
    Stop words
    Very common words such as the, and and for. They're left out of the repeated words list.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is this word counter free?

    Yes. This word counter is completely free, with no signup, no trial and no limit on how many times you use it. There's no watermark and no ads inside the tool. Paste your text, read the counts, and copy or download the text if you need it. Nothing needs to be installed.

    Is my text safe? Is it uploaded anywhere?

    Your text is processed in your browser and is never uploaded. The page counts words with JavaScript on your device. It doesn't send your text to a server or save it in cookies or local storage. Closing or refreshing the tab clears the box, so keep a copy of anything you need.

    How does the tool count words?

    It splits your text at spaces and line breaks, then counts every piece that contains at least one letter or number. So well-known and don't count as one word each, while a lone dash or emoji doesn't count. Numbers such as 2026 count as words, the way most word processors treat them.

    What is the difference between characters with and without spaces?

    Characters with spaces counts everything you typed, including spaces, tabs and line breaks. Characters without spaces counts only visible characters. Use the first for limits on social posts and texts, and the second when an assignment or translation job asks for characters excluding spaces. Most emoji count as one character, but combined emoji can count as several.

    How is reading time calculated?

    Reading time divides your word count by a reading speed, 200 words per minute by default. Speaking time uses 130 words per minute. You can edit both speeds, because people read and talk at different paces. Treat the result as an estimate for planning, not a precise measurement.

    Is there a limit on how much text I can count?

    You can paste or type as much as your browser handles comfortably. Text files you load are limited to 2 MB, which is roughly two million plain English characters. Very large text may make the page respond more slowly on older phones, because the stats update whenever the text changes.

    Does it count words in other languages?

    It works best with languages that put spaces between words, such as English, Spanish, French and German. Chinese, Japanese and Thai are usually written without spaces, so a run of characters counts as a single word. The character counts stay accurate for those languages, so use them instead.

    Does it work on my phone?

    Yes. The layout is built for small screens first, the buttons are at least 44 pixels tall, and the stats update as you type or paste. Use the Paste text button to bring in what you copied, or long-press the box to paste. Everything runs in your phone's browser.

    Does it work offline?

    Yes, once the page has loaded. Counting runs in your browser, so it keeps working if your connection drops. You need to be online the first time you open the page, and the tool doesn't install itself as an offline app. Reloading the page while offline may not work.

    Why does my word count differ from Microsoft Word or Google Docs?

    Counting rules differ. This tool counts any space-separated piece that contains a letter or number. Word processors may count standalone dashes or symbols, treat hyphenated or slashed terms differently, or include footnotes and headers. A difference of a few words is normal. For submissions, trust the count your recipient uses.
